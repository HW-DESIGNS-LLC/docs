ESPHome · Home Assistant

Your board in Home Assistant

Install ESPHome from this page and Crescent City becomes a Home Assistant device: indoor temperature and humidity, battery level, USB power, the BOOT button and the RGB LED all show up as entities — while the OLED keeps showing the time, the temperature inside and out, and the battery.

Installing from this page needs Chrome or Edge on a computer. You can still use the ESPHome Builder steps below. Installing needs a secure (https) connection to this page. View the YAML ↗

ESPHome 2026.9+ · Home Assistant · board V1.0 and V2.0

Install

Two ways to get ESPHome on the board

New to ESPHome? Use option A — it takes about two minutes and needs nothing but a browser. Already run ESPHome Builder? Option B adds the board like any other device.

Option A · From this page
  1. 1

    Connect the board

    Plug the board into your computer with a data-capable USB‑C cable. Open this page in Chrome or Edge.

  2. 2

    Click Install ESPHome

    Pick the board’s USB port in the list, confirm, and leave the box ticked to erase the board on a first install. Installing takes about a minute.

    This replaces the weather clock. You can put the weather clock back at any time — see Help.

  3. 3

    Connect it to your Wi‑Fi

    When the install finishes, choose Connect to Wi‑Fi and enter your network and password.

    No computer at hand later? With no saved network the board opens a setup hotspot called Crescent City. Join it from your phone and pick your network on the page that opens. Use a 2.4 GHz network.

  4. 4

    Add it in Home Assistant

    Home Assistant finds the board by itself: go to Settings → Devices & services, look for Crescent City under Discovered and click Add. The clock on the OLED starts as soon as Home Assistant is connected.

    Not listed after a minute? Click Add integration, choose ESPHome and enter the IP address from the board’s status page (press BOOT). Leave the port at 6053.

  5. 5

    Optional: take it over in ESPHome Builder

    To change the configuration or update over Wi‑Fi, open ESPHome Builder: the board appears as Discovered. Click Adopt, then Install. Builder keeps your copy linked to the published configuration.

Option B · ESPHome Builder
  1. 1

    Create the device

    In ESPHome Builder click New device, give it a name, choose ESP32‑S3, and skip the first install. Open Edit and replace everything except the api: block with:

    substitutions:
  name: crescent-city
  friendly_name: Crescent City

packages:
  hwdesigns.crescent-city: github://HW-DESIGNS-LLC/docs/esp32-s3/firmware/esphome/crescent-city.yaml@main

esphome:
  name: ${name}
  name_add_mac_suffix: false

wifi:
  ssid: !secret wifi_ssid
  password: !secret wifi_password
  2. 2

    Install over USB the first time

    Click Install and choose the USB option. After that, updates go over Wi‑Fi. Then add the device in Home Assistant as in option A, step 4.

    If the port doesn’t appear, hold the BOOT button while plugging the board in.

What you get

Entities in Home Assistant

Everything the board can sense or show, ready for dashboards and automations.

EntityWhat it does
Indoor temperature, Indoor humidityFrom a Qwiic SHT4x sensor on the side connector, every 30 s. Plug the sensor in before powering up.
Battery voltageVoltage at the battery connector, a 30‑second median reported every 30 s. With no battery connected it shows the charger output, about 4.1 V.
Battery levelCharge in % from the battery voltage. Reads high while USB is plugged in and charging.
Battery lowTurns on when the battery needs charging (below 3.45 V). You can use it in your own Home Assistant automations.
USB powerOn while the board is powered from USB‑C.
BOOT buttonOn while the button is pressed. Each press also switches the OLED between the clock and status pages.
RGB LEDA colour light you can control. Turn the Status LED switch off first so the board doesn’t use it for status.
Status LED (switch)Lets the RGB LED show connection and battery status (below).
Low-battery shutdown (switch)On by default. See Battery.
Wi‑Fi signal, IP address, UptimeDiagnostics.
Restart, Factory resetButtons. Factory reset is hidden by default; it clears saved Wi‑Fi and settings.

On the OLED

Status LED

Red blinkBattery low — plug in USB
Blue blinkNot on Wi‑Fi yet, or the setup hotspot is open
AmberOn Wi‑Fi, waiting for Home Assistant
OffAll good

Battery

Runs on a LiPo, and knows when to stop

On battery, the board watches its own supply and shuts down cleanly before the battery runs flat. While USB is plugged in, none of this applies.

Nearly empty

Below 3.45 V the battery icon blinks with LOW, the status LED blinks red, and Battery low turns on in Home Assistant.

Empty

Below 3.30 V the OLED shows Battery low — charge me for 10 seconds. Then Wi‑Fi, the LED and the screen switch off and the board sleeps.

Back on

Plug in USB‑C: the board wakes, starts normally and charges. Pressing RESET on a still-empty battery just shows the message again.

The decision uses a 30‑second median, so a short dip during a Wi‑Fi burst can’t trigger it. The same thresholds are used by the weather clock firmware. Turning the Low-battery shutdown switch off disables the warning and the shutdown — not recommended on a battery.

Customize

Make it yours

After adopting the board in ESPHome Builder, change any of these by adding a substitutions: block to your copy of the configuration, then install.

temp_unit"F" or "C" — OLED only; match your Home Assistant unit setting
outdoor_weather_entityweather.forecast_home — the weather entity for the outdoor temperature
friendly_nameCrescent City — the device name in Home Assistant
vbat_warn / vbat_cutoff3.45 / 3.30 — battery thresholds in volts (advanced)
substitutions:
  temp_unit: "C"
  outdoor_weather_entity: weather.home

Free header pins for your own sensors and outputs: GPIO 1, 5, 6, 7, 13, 15, 16, 21, 35, 36, 37 and 39 — see the pinout. The Qwiic port takes more I²C sensors on the same bus.

Help

Common questions

The Install button doesn’t find the board

Use a data-capable USB‑C cable and Chrome or Edge on a computer. If the port still doesn’t appear, hold the BOOT button while plugging the board in, release it, and click Install again.

The clock shows --:--

The board takes the time and time zone from Home Assistant. It appears once the device is added in Home Assistant (status page: HA connected).

Outdoor temperature shows --

The board reads the temperature of weather.forecast_home, the weather entity Home Assistant creates for your home. If yours has a different name, set outdoor_weather_entity (see Customize).

Indoor temperature is unavailable

Plug a Qwiic SHT4x sensor into the side connector and restart the board — ESPHome looks for the sensor at power-up.

How do I get the weather clock back?

Upload the weather clock sketch from the Arduino IDE, as in the getting-started guide. Its source is in the firmware folder.

Does it need Home Assistant?

For the clock and the outdoor temperature, yes. Everything else — sensors, battery, the setup hotspot — works on the board itself, and ESPHome devices can also be used without Home Assistant.
